## Repetition review

The original basic/business-context pairs in the revenue families remain largely cosmetic repetitions of a recognition rule. Clinic/workshop rent repeat current occupancy; planned equipment/payroll borrowing and share issuance repeat source versus intended use. Later additions supply timing, role, entity perspective, separate recorded balances, partial/final amounts and noncash performance cues rather than only renamed businesses.

Paid-in-full advances overlap full morning receipts; their distinctive fact is a misleading receipt label. Early and overdue invoice collections share the same rule and differ in deadline direction. Mistaken cash-sale and insurance-budget labels share facts-over-label reasoning. Partial/final prepaid consumption and partial/final earning share portion-versus-original-total reasoning. Additional equipment overlaps replacement equipment with the old machine retained; stockholder consulting dividends overlap working-owner dividends. These are deliberate transfer variations, not new accounting rules. No exact duplicates does not mean no semantic repetition. Retain published versions at this checkpoint; no retirement or silent rewrite is justified by cosmetic similarity alone without reviewing selection and delayed transfer.

Reviewed comparisons include morning receipt/evening performance, third-party current earning/old claim collection, customer-as-investor/stockholder-as-customer, personally held customer currency/company-held currency, and early settlement/future-performance advance. Tests reject balanced entries from the wrong event family. These partners are documented/reviewed; the scheduler does not automatically select these exact matched pairs.
