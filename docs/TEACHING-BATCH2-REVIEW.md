## CashRent

| Stage | Prompt | Hint | Explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Which account records the cost of using the premises this month? | Does today's payment buy future use of the premises or pay for the current month's use? | Rent Expense records the current month's occupancy cost. Prepaid Rent would represent future use, and Accounts Payable would represent an amount still owed; this rent was paid today. |
| S2 | What category of account is Rent Expense? | Does this account track a future resource, an obligation, or a cost incurred in operating the business? | Rent Expense is an Expense: a cost of using the premises this month. It reduces equity through income; it is not an asset providing future use. |
| S3 | Does the expense account balance increase or decrease when recording this rent payment? | Does recognizing this month's rent add to the costs accumulated this period or remove a previously recorded cost? | Rent Expense increases as this month's cost is recorded. Cash decreases, but that does not make the expense account decrease. |
| S4 | How is an increase in an Expense (Rent Expense) recorded? | An account increases on its normal-balance side. Which side is an expense's normal balance? | Expenses have a normal debit balance, so an increase is a Debit (left side). Expenses reduce equity, but the expense account itself increases; credit would decrease it. |
| S5 | Which other account changes when the company pays for this month's use of the premises? | Did the company hand over money today or only promise to pay later? | Cash decreases because the company paid today, so Cash is credited. Recording a payable instead would leave the already-paid amount outstanding. |
| S6 | What is the complete balanced journal entry for paying cash rent? | The premises were used this month and paid for today. What cost was incurred, and what resource was given up? | Debit Rent Expense ${amount} and Credit Cash ${amount}. The cost belongs to this month, so treating it as a prepayment would defer a cost already incurred; a payable would ignore today's payment. |
| S7 | How does paying cash rent affect the accounting equation? | Did the payment leave the company with a new future resource, or was this month's benefit already used? | Assets decrease by ${amount} (Cash) and equity decreases by ${amount} through Rent Expense. No debt was settled: the rent was not previously owed. The expense increases even though equity decreases. |

## BorrowCash

| Stage | Prompt | Hint | Explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | What did the company receive today, and which account records it? | What came into the business from the lender today? | Cash records the money received. Notes Payable records the separate repayment obligation; Service Revenue would imply earning from work, which borrowing does not create. |
| S2 | What category of account is Cash? | Is cash a resource the company owns, an amount it owes, or an ownership claim? | Cash is an Asset because the company owns and can use it. Cash is not Revenue: earning, borrowing, and owner investment can all bring in cash. |
| S3 | Does Cash increase or decrease upon borrowing money? | Compare the money held before and after today's receipt. Is there more or less? | Cash increases because money arrived today. A future repayment obligation does not cancel today's receipt. |
| S4 | How is an increase to an Asset (Cash) recorded? | An account increases on its normal-balance side. Which side is an asset's normal balance? | Assets have a normal debit balance, so an increase is a Debit (left side). A credit would decrease the asset; debit does not mean money came in. |
| S5 | Which account balances the receipt from the lender? | Did the lender buy ownership, pay for work, or expect the money back? | Notes Payable records the signed promise to repay, so it is credited as the liability increases. Service Revenue would claim earning; Common Stock would claim an ownership investment. Neither happened. |
| S6 | What is the complete balanced journal entry for borrowing cash on a note? | The company received money under a signed loan agreement. What did it gain, and what must it do later? | Debit Cash ${amount} and Credit Notes Payable ${amount}. Cash increased and a debt was created. Crediting Service Revenue instead would report borrowing as income. |
| S7 | How does borrowing cash affect the accounting equation? | Does receiving a loan create earning, an ownership contribution, or a repayment obligation? | Assets increase by ${amount} (Cash) and liabilities increase by ${amount} (Notes Payable). Equity is unchanged because the cash was borrowed, not earned or contributed by owners. |

| S2 | What category of account is Notes Payable? | Does a signed promise to repay the bank describe a resource owned or an obligation owed? | Notes Payable is a Liability: principal the company must repay. | S3 | Does Notes Payable increase or decrease when principal is repaid? | After repaying part of the principal, does the company owe the bank more or less? | Notes Payable decreases as principal is settled. Borrowing would increase the debt, but today's event is repayment. |

| S5 | Which other account changes when the company pays principal today? | Did the company hand over money today or only promise to pay later? | Cash decreases because the company paid today, so Cash is credited. Recording a payable instead would leave the already-paid amount outstanding. |

| S5 | Which other account changes when this newly declared distribution is paid today? | Did the company hand over money today or only promise to pay later? | Cash decreases because the company paid today, so Cash is credited.
