## Repetition and transfer review

Fifty variants cover thirteen canonical recognition rules. Exact text uniqueness does not establish semantic novelty. The basic and business-context pairs in the five original revenue/timing families largely repeat the same reasoning. The coordinated workshop trio and repair pair are useful comparison material, but do not add new accounting rules. Current rent examples share the current-cost cue. Planned payroll and planned equipment examples in borrowing/share issuance repeat the distinction between cash source and intended later use.

Later batches add meaningful facts: unrelated prior balances, incremental receipts, partial settlement or performance, counterparty roles, resource identity, acquisition before use, consumption without a claim, and complete principal settlement. These distinctions require the learner to select today's event and amount without repeating earlier recognition. They improve transfer coverage while retaining the same canonical entries. Future additions should document that distinction explicitly; business renaming alone should not fill targets.

Matched contrasts have been reviewed and tested, but automatic contrast scheduling is still deferred. The existing mastery/evidence logic is unchanged. Counts alone do not demonstrate mastery, and no learner history was evaluated for this checkpoint. Review provenance records delegated assistant review; it does not imply a fresh independent human review of every scenario.

Every active scenario has three allowed amounts. The 32 expansion additions use $50/$100/$200. Older scenarios retain several larger amount sets, as published; their values and versions were not silently normalized. This is a consistency follow-up if the trivial-dollar convention is later applied to existing content, rather than evidence of additional scenario diversity.
